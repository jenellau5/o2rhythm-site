After an argument

How to calm down after an argument

The argument is over, but your body didn't get the message. Your heart is still going, you keep replaying what they said, and you're writing comebacks in your head. That's normal. Here's what actually helps in the next few minutes.

Why "just calm down" doesn't work

In a heated argument, your body reacts the way it would to a threat. Your heart speeds up, your breathing gets shallow, and stress hormones keep you on alert. That doesn't switch off the moment the yelling stops. It can take 20 minutes or longer to wear off.

While it's running, it's harder to listen, harder to think straight, and much easier to say the thing you'll regret. That's why round two of an argument is usually worse than round one.

Telling yourself to calm down rarely works, because the problem isn't only in your head. It's in your body. So start there.

Step 1 · About 2 minutes

Calm your body first

Pick one of these. You don't need all of them.

Breathe out longer than you breathe in

Breathe in through your nose for a count of 4, then out slowly through your mouth for 6 to 8. A long exhale is one of the quickest ways to tell your body the danger has passed. Another version: take two short breaths in through your nose, then one long, slow breath out. Repeat for a minute or two.

Move it out

Walk around the block, shake out your hands and arms, or press your palms together hard for 10 seconds and then let go. Your body got ready for a fight, and moving helps burn that off.

Cold water

Splash cold water on your face or run it over your wrists. It's a fast way to interrupt the spiral.

Take space, and say you're coming back

If you're still in the same room, it's okay to say, "I need 20 minutes, and then I want to finish this." Stepping away to calm down isn't the same as walking out.

Step 2 · Before you text anything

Get it out somewhere safe

You probably have a whole speech building up. Write it down somewhere they'll never see it. All of it, unfiltered. Then answer a few questions:

  • What am I most upset about, really?
  • What did I need in that moment that I didn't get?
  • Is any part of this mine? (It's okay if the answer is no.)

The point isn't to send it. It's so the unfiltered version doesn't come out in a text at midnight. Most people find that once it's written down, it loses a lot of its grip.

Step 3 · When you're ready

Decide what to do next

Not every argument has to be settled tonight. Before you reach out, ask yourself one thing: am I calm enough to listen, not just to talk? If not, give it more time.

When you are ready, short is better than long. A few ways to start:

  • "I'm not ready to talk yet, but I'm not ignoring you. Can we talk tomorrow?"
  • "I didn't like how I said that. I'm sorry for that part."
  • "I still want to talk about this, just calmer. When's good for you?"

And if they're not someone you can talk to right now, reach someone else. A friend who will just listen, without picking a side, can help more than you'd expect.

Common questions

How long does it take to calm down after an argument?

Your body can take 20 minutes or more to settle after a heated argument, even once the words have stopped. Slow breathing with a long exhale and a little movement can help it settle faster.

Should I text them right after an argument?

Usually it's better to wait until you're calm enough to listen, not just reply. If you want them to know you're not ignoring them, a short message like "I need a little time, but I'm not going anywhere" is enough for now.

Is it okay to walk away during an argument?

Yes, as long as you say you're coming back. Taking a break to calm down is different from walking out. Something like "I need 20 minutes and then I want to finish this" keeps the door open.

If arguments feel scary, out of control, or physical, that's more than a breathing exercise can fix, and you deserve real support. You can reach the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788. If you're thinking about hurting yourself, call or text 988. In an emergency, call 911.
