When you feel overwhelmed

What to do when you feel overwhelmed

Too much at once. Too many people needing something, too many things going wrong, too many tabs open in your head. You can't think, so you can't start, so it piles up even more. Here's how to break that loop.

Why you freeze

When too much is coming in, your brain can't sort it all, so it treats the whole pile as one big threat. That's why being overwhelmed often feels like being stuck. You know you need to do something, but you can't pick what.

The fix isn't to try harder. It's to shrink things down.

Step 1 · About 2 minutes

Turn the volume down

Step away from screens

Put your phone face down and close the laptop for two minutes. Fewer things coming in gives your brain room.

Breathe out longer than you breathe in

In for 4, out for 6 to 8. Repeat until your shoulders drop a little.

5, 4, 3, 2, 1

Name 5 things you can see, 4 you can feel, 3 you can hear, 2 you can smell and 1 you can taste. It pulls your attention into right now.

Step 2 · Brain dump

Get it all out of your head

Write down everything that's on you right now. All of it, big and small, in any order. Don't organize it yet. Then go back and mark:

  • What has to happen today?
  • What can wait, or be done by someone else?
  • What isn't actually mine to carry?

Seeing it on paper usually makes it smaller than it felt in your head.

Step 3 · Then

Pick one thing

Not the most important thing. Just the next thing, something you can do in 10 minutes. Do that, then pick the next one.

And ask for help. Overwhelm grows when you try to do everything alone.

  • "I'm underwater right now. Could you take this one thing off my plate?"
  • "Can this wait until Thursday? I want to do it right."
  • "I'm overwhelmed. I don't need advice, I just need to say it out loud to someone."

Common questions

How do I calm down when I'm overwhelmed?

Cut down what's coming in for a couple of minutes, put your phone down, and breathe out slowly. Then write everything down so it isn't all living in your head at once.

What should I do first when everything feels like too much?

Pick the smallest next step you can finish in about 10 minutes. Finishing one small thing breaks the freeze and makes the next step easier.

Why do I shut down when I'm overwhelmed?

When your brain gets more than it can sort, it can go into a kind of freeze. It isn't laziness. Reducing what's coming in and shrinking the task usually gets you moving again.

If feeling overwhelmed has become most days, or you're having trouble eating, sleeping or getting through the day, that's worth talking about with a doctor or counselor. If you feel like you can't go on, call or text 988 any time.
