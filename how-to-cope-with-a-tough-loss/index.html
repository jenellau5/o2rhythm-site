After a tough loss

How to cope with a tough loss

You lost the game. You didn't make the team. You didn't get the job, the part, the spot, the yes. It stings, and it might feel bigger than anyone around you seems to get. Here's how to let it settle without stuffing it down.

Why it hurts this much

When you work hard for something, you put part of yourself into it. Losing can feel like a judgment on you, not just a result. Your body reacts too: the tight chest, the lump in your throat, the urge to be anywhere else.

That's disappointment, and it needs a few minutes to move through before you can think about what's next.

Step 1 · Right after

Let your body settle

Breathe it down

In for 4, out slowly for 6 to 8. A longer breath out helps your body come down from the adrenaline of competing or waiting.

Give it somewhere to go

Go for a walk or a run, shoot around alone, or hit a pillow. Moving helps the frustration leave your body.

Find a private spot

It's okay to cry in the car, a bathroom stall or a stairwell. Holding it in all day is harder than letting it out for five minutes.

Step 2 · Later that day

Get it out

Write privately, just for you:

  • What did I want so badly?
  • What am I most afraid this means?
  • What did I do well, even though it didn't go my way?

Watch for the jump from "I lost" to "I'm not good enough." One result is information. It isn't a verdict on who you are.

Step 3 · When you're ready, not tonight

Figure out what's next

  • Is there something specific to learn? Once you're calm, ask the coach or interviewer for one piece of feedback.
  • Who can I talk to who won't just say "it's fine"?
  • What do I still have, or still want to work toward?
  • "I didn't make it. I don't need advice right now, I just wanted you to know."
  • "Could you tell me one thing I could work on for next time?"
  • "Can we do something normal tonight? I need a break from thinking about it."

If you're a parent reading this for your kid: the most helpful thing is usually to say less. "That really hurts. I'm proud of how hard you worked." Then let them lead.

Common questions

How do I get over losing a big game?

Let yourself feel it for a bit instead of pushing it away. Move your body, talk to a teammate or a friend, and wait until the next day to break down what went wrong. Most athletes find the sting fades faster when they don't pretend it isn't there.

What should I do after not making the team?

Give it a day, then ask the coach for one or two specific things to work on. Decide whether you want to try again, play somewhere else, or put that energy into something new. All three are good answers.

How do I deal with not getting a job I really wanted?

Let yourself be disappointed, then ask for feedback if you can. Write down what you learned about what you want. A no from one place says more about fit and timing than about your worth.

If a loss is leaving you feeling hopeless for days, or like nothing matters, talk to someone you trust. You can also call or text 988 any time, day or night.
