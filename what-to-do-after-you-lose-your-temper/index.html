After you lose your temper

What to do after you lose your temper

You said it, or did it, and you can't take it back. Maybe you're still angry. Maybe you've already flipped to feeling awful about it. Either way, one bad moment doesn't make you a bad person. Here's how to get steady first, and then how to make it right.

Why it hits so hard afterward

When you lose your temper, your body goes into full fight mode. Once it passes, a lot of people swing straight into shame: replaying it, calling themselves names, wanting to disappear. Both are your nervous system still running hot.

Trying to fix things from there usually makes them worse. So the order matters: calm down first, then make sense of it, then repair.

Step 1 · About 2 minutes

Get out of fight mode

Step away

If you can, leave the room for a few minutes. If you need to say something, keep it short: "I need a minute. I'll be back."

Breathe out longer than you breathe in

In through your nose for 4, out slowly through your mouth for 6 to 8. Do it for a minute or two. A long exhale is one of the fastest ways to bring your heart rate down.

Unclench on purpose

Notice your jaw, your fists, your shoulders. Squeeze them tight for 5 seconds, then let go all at once. Do it twice. It's easier to relax a muscle after you've tightened it.

Cold water

Splash your face or run cold water over your wrists. It interrupts the heat quickly.

Step 2 · Once you're calmer

Make sense of it, without beating yourself up

Write down what happened, privately. Not to judge yourself, just to understand it.

  • What set me off, right before I snapped?
  • What was I already carrying today? Tired, hungry, stressed, hurt?
  • What did I actually want in that moment?
  • What would I do differently next time?

Notice the difference between "I did something I regret" and "I'm a terrible person." You can fix the first one. The second one just keeps you stuck.

Step 3 · When you're ready

Make it right

A real apology is short, specific, and doesn't explain the behavior away. A few ways to start:

  • "I'm sorry I yelled at you. You didn't deserve that."
  • "I lost my temper earlier and I'm sorry. I'm working on it."
  • "I said something that wasn't fair. Can we talk when you're ready?"

Skip "I'm sorry, but." The "but" cancels the apology. And give them time. They might need a while to calm down too.

Common questions

Why do I feel so bad after I get angry?

Part of it is your body coming down from a rush of stress hormones, which can leave you shaky and drained. Part of it is that you care about the person. Guilt that leads you to repair things is useful. Shame that keeps you stuck isn't.

How do I apologize after losing my temper?

Keep it short and specific. Say what you did, skip the excuses, and say what you'll do differently. Then give the other person time to respond in their own way.

How can I stop losing my temper?

Learn your early signs, like a hot face, a tight jaw or talking faster, and step away at that point instead of later. Look at what's underneath, like sleep and stress. If it keeps happening, a counselor or an anger management class can really help.

If your anger has led to hurting someone or breaking things, or the people around you feel afraid, talking with a counselor is a strong next step. The National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-7233) also talks with people who are worried about their own behavior. If you're thinking about hurting yourself, call or text 988. In an emergency, call 911.
