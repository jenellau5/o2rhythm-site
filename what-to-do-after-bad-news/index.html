After bad news

What to do right after you get bad news

A phone call, a text, a test result, a conversation you didn't see coming. Your stomach drops and everything feels a little unreal. You don't have to figure it all out right now. You only have to get through the next few minutes.

What's happening in your body

Shock does strange things. Some people cry right away. Some go numb, or oddly calm, or can't stop moving. Some feel like they're watching themselves from the outside.

All of that is normal. Your mind is trying to take in something big, and it needs time.

Step 1 · The first few minutes

Get steady

Sit down if you can

Put your feet flat on the floor and both hands on something solid, like a table or your knees.

Breathe out slowly

You don't need a pattern. Just make each breath out a little longer than the one before.

Name what you see

Five things, out loud or in your head. It helps when everything feels unreal.

Drink some water

Small, ordinary things help your body feel safer.

Step 2 · When your mind is racing

Get it out of your head

You might have a hundred questions at once. Write them down so you don't have to hold them all.

  • What do I actually know right now?
  • What don't I know yet?
  • What's the one thing that needs to happen today, if anything?

Most bad news doesn't need a decision in the first hour. It's okay to say, "I'll think about that tomorrow."

Step 3 · When you're ready

Let someone in

You don't have to explain everything. A short message is enough so you're not alone with it:

  • "I just got some hard news. Can you call me?"
  • "I'm not ready to talk about it, but can you just be with me for a bit?"
  • "Something happened. I'll tell you more later. I just needed someone to know."

Common questions

How do I calm down after getting bad news?

Sit down, put your feet on the floor, and slow your breathing, making each breath out a little longer. Then name a few things around you. It helps your body catch up with what your mind just heard.

Why do I feel numb after bad news?

Numbness is a common first reaction to shock. It's your mind giving you a little distance while it takes things in. Feelings often arrive later, sometimes in waves, and that's normal too.

What should I do first after bad news?

Get steady, then write down what you know and what you don't. Decide whether anything truly has to happen today. Then let one person know, even with a short text.

If the news has you thinking about hurting yourself, or you can't keep yourself safe, call or text 988 now, or call 911.
